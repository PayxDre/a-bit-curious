Bitcoin vs Other Cryptos

Bitcoin vs everything else: founder stakes, supply changes, outages, and the one decentralization test most coins fail.

Open the interactive lesson →

⚠️ Critical distinction: Bitcoin is not "a cryptocurrency." Bitcoin is the cryptocurrency. Everything else is a fundamentally different thing wearing similar clothing. Understanding why will save you from costly mistakes.

There are over 20,000 cryptocurrencies. Most people assume they're all variations of the same idea - like different brands of the same product. This is the single most dangerous misunderstanding in the entire space. Bitcoin was created to be sound money - a replacement for the broken fiat system. The vast majority of other cryptocurrencies were created to make their founders rich. This isn't cynicism; it's a verifiable structural difference that shows up in how they're built, governed, and distributed.

For each of the top cryptocurrencies, ask yourself: Who created it? Who controls it? Can they change the rules? Can they freeze your funds? Bitcoin's answers are unique: no one, no one, no, and no. Let's go through the biggest names one by one.

Ethereum has no fixed supply cap. Its monetary policy has been changed at least five times by its core developers. In 2016, they rolled back the entire blockchain to reverse a hack (the DAO hack), effectively reversing finalized transactions. The Ethereum Foundation raised $18 million in a pre-sale where early participants bought tokens before the public launch. 72 million ETH were pre-mined - roughly 60% of the initial supply. Vitalik Buterin and a small group of core developers control the roadmap, including the 2022 switch from Proof of Work to Proof of Stake. Unlike Bitcoin, Ethereum's monetary policy, consensus mechanism, and technical direction can be - and have been - changed by a small group of people.

Tether claims each USDT is backed 1:1 by US dollars, but has never completed a full independent audit. The CFTC fined Tether $41 million in 2021 for misrepresenting its reserves - at one point, as little as 74% was actually dollar-backed. Tether Limited can mint unlimited new tokens at will and has frozen over $1.5 billion in USDT across hundreds of addresses at the request of law enforcement and governments. When an address is blacklisted, those funds become permanently unmovable on-chain. USDT is issued and controlled by a single private company (Tether Limited / iFinex). By contrast, Bitcoin has no issuing company and no mechanism to freeze funds.

100 billion XRP tokens were pre-created at launch - none were mined. Ripple Labs retained over 60% of the total supply and has been selling into the market for over a decade. Co-founder Jed McCaleb sold roughly $3 billion worth of XRP after leaving the company. The SEC sued Ripple in 2020 for selling unregistered securities. The network operates on a "Unique Node List" of trusted validators, which Ripple has significant influence over. Unlike Bitcoin's permissionless mining, XRP's supply was entirely pre-allocated and its validation is restricted to approved participants.

BNB was created by the Binance exchange. 100% of the supply was created by the company, with 40% allocated to the team and founders. The BNB Chain operates with 21 validators, all selected by Binance. The chain has been halted multiple times by Binance's decision. Its founder, Changpeng Zhao (CZ), pled guilty to federal money laundering charges in 2023 and served prison time. Quarterly token "burns" are controlled entirely by Binance - the company decides how much supply to remove and when. BNB's supply, validation, and governance are all controlled by a single company.

Solana has experienced 7+ total network outages where the chain stopped producing blocks entirely. Each time, validator operators coordinated a restart through private channels. About 48% of the initial token supply went to insiders and venture capital firms at discounted rates - including FTX, whose founder Sam Bankman-Fried was later convicted of fraud. Solana has no hard supply cap and has ~4.6% annual inflation. Its validator set is concentrated among a relatively small number of well-funded operators. Bitcoin, by comparison, has never experienced an outage in 17 years of operation.

USDC is a stablecoin issued by Circle, a private company. Like Tether, Circle has the ability to freeze and blacklist addresses, and has done so on multiple occasions. During the 2023 Silicon Valley Bank collapse, Circle had $3.3 billion in reserves held at SVB, causing USDC to temporarily de-peg to $0.87. The value of USDC depends entirely on Circle's reserve management and banking relationships. Circle controls issuance, redemption, and can render any address's USDC non-transferable. Bitcoin has no equivalent single point of failure.

Dogecoin was created in 2013 as a parody of Bitcoin. It has no supply cap: roughly 5 billion new DOGE are minted every year with no end date. Development activity has been minimal for long stretches, and there is no formal roadmap. Co-founder Billy Markus sold his holdings early on. The token's market price has been heavily influenced by social media activity, particularly tweets from Elon Musk. Dogecoin uses Proof of Work (Scrypt) and is merge-mined with Litecoin. Unlike Bitcoin's disinflationary design, Dogecoin's perpetual inflation means the supply grows indefinitely.

Cardano was founded by Charles Hoskinson, a former Ethereum co-founder. It raised funds through an ICO and allocated ~16% of the supply to three organizations: IOHK, Emurgo, and the Cardano Foundation. Cardano emphasizes a "peer-reviewed" academic approach to development. After 8+ years, its smart contract functionality arrived later than initially planned, and on-chain activity remains modest compared to other platforms. Development is guided primarily by IOHK (now IOG), which Hoskinson leads. Cardano uses Proof of Stake (Ouroboros). Unlike Bitcoin, its roadmap and development priorities are set by identifiable organizations rather than decentralized consensus.

TRON was created by Justin Sun. Its original white paper contained sections that closely matched text from Ethereum and other projects. The SEC charged Sun with fraud and market manipulation in 2023, alleging wash trading and undisclosed celebrity endorsements of TRX. Sun has significant control over the TRON Foundation and network direction. He acquired BitTorrent for $140 million in 2018. TRON's block production is handled by 27 Super Representatives elected through a delegated Proof of Stake system. Unlike Bitcoin's ~60,000 independent nodes, TRON's governance is concentrated among a small number of block producers.

Litecoin is a fork of Bitcoin's code with faster block times (2.5 minutes vs. 10) and a different hashing algorithm (Scrypt vs. SHA-256). Its creator, Charlie Lee, sold all of his LTC holdings near the all-time high in December 2017, citing a desire to avoid conflicts of interest. Litecoin had a fair launch with no pre-mine. Development activity has slowed in recent years, and the project has not introduced major technical differentiators beyond its original modifications to Bitcoin's design. It is sometimes referred to as "digital silver" to Bitcoin's "digital gold."

⚠️ Case Study: Zcash and the Invisible Counterfeit (June 2026)

On June 5, 2026, the Zcash project disclosed that a critical bug had been hiding in its Orchard shielded pool since the NU5 upgrade in May 2022 - four years undetected. The flaw was an under-constrained element in the circuit's elliptic-curve multiplication gadget: an arithmetic check on transaction inputs contained a gap that allowed arbitrary false values to pass. Anyone who found it could have minted unlimited counterfeit ZEC inside the shielded pool, completely invisibly.

Security researcher Taylor Hornby discovered the vulnerability on May 29, 2026 using an AI-assisted auditing framework built on Anthropic's Opus 4.8 model. Working in a local test environment, Hornby wrote a complete exploit that generated unlimited, undetectable counterfeit ZEC - confirming the bug was not theoretical. Zcash developers executed an emergency hard fork (NU6.2) by June 3 and disabled the Orchard pool before publicly disclosing on June 5. ZEC crashed 38%.

Supply hidden by design. When a counterfeiting bug exists, nobody can tell if it was exploited. Zcash founder Zooko Wilcox acknowledged users "cannot independently verify whether ZEC's circulating supply was affected."

Every satoshi is tracked in a public ledger. Anyone running a node can verify the total supply at any time. A counterfeiting bug in Bitcoin would be immediately visible to tens of thousands of auditors.

This is why Bitcoin's radical transparency is a strength, not a weakness. Privacy is important - but when privacy means even the monetary supply itself becomes unknowable, you have recreated the exact problem Bitcoin was built to solve: money you cannot verify, backed by trust you cannot audit.

🔮 The Decentralization Test - Who Passes?

Score based on: no pre-mine, no foundation control, supply immutability, network uptime, permissionless validation, no rollbacks, no fund-freezing capability. Only Bitcoin scores 100%.

The track record speaks for itself. Bitcoin has operated continuously since January 3, 2009 - no outages, no rollbacks, no frozen funds, no supply changes. Governments have attempted to ban it in multiple countries; the network continued uninterrupted. By contrast, Ethereum has rolled back its blockchain, Solana has gone offline 7+ times, Tether and USDC have frozen user funds, and XRP was the subject of an SEC enforcement action. Each of these projects involves identifiable founders, companies, or foundations that control key decisions. Bitcoin is the only major cryptocurrency where no individual, company, or organization controls the supply, the rules, or the network.

Proof of Work and Proof of Stake determine who controls the network and how power is distributed over time.
