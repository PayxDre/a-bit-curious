Public Keys

One-way cryptography you can feel: derive addresses instantly, then try to reverse one and see why you cannot.

Open the interactive lesson →

A public key is derived from your private key using elliptic curve multiplication . Think of it like mixing paint: starting with a secret color, you apply a recipe to get a new color. Anyone can see the result, but they can't figure out the original.

Public keys are 33 bytes (compressed). From your public key, your address is derived via SHA-256 then RIPEMD-160 .

📢 From Public Key to Address

Public keys are safe to share. After spending from an address, your public key is revealed, so best practice is to use each address only once for privacy.

The relationship: private key is your identity proof, public key is your verifiable credential, address is your shareable contact info. None of this requires a passport, a bank account, or anyone's permission.
