Digital Signatures

Real ECDSA in your browser: sign a message, tamper with it, and watch verification catch every single change.

Open the interactive lesson →

A digital signature is like signing a check - but mathematically impossible to forge. Your wallet takes your private key and transaction data and runs them through ECDSA (recently upgraded to Schnorr signatures via Taproot).

Every signature is unique, even for the same private key. Yet anyone with your public key can instantly verify it - without knowing your private key. This verification is what every node does for every new transaction.

✍️ How Digital Signing Works

Signatures bind three things: the private key (who you are), the transaction (what you authorized), and the signature (the proof). Change even a single byte of the transaction after signing and the signature becomes invalid.

In 2021, Taproot brought Schnorr signatures, enabling "signature aggregation" - combining many signatures into one. Multi-signature transactions became cheaper and indistinguishable from regular transactions.
