6 Myths Debunked

The lies you've been told, corrected · ~4 min read

The classic Bitcoin myths - crime, energy, bans, no intrinsic value - examined against the actual data. This is the reading version - the interactive lesson has the live demos, quizzes, and progress tracking.

Start the interactive lesson →

Every revolutionary technology faces resistance dressed up as "common sense." The car was called a death machine. The internet was dismissed as a fad. Bitcoin's critics recycle the same tired myths - and every one of them collapses under scrutiny.

MYTH #1 "Bitcoin has no intrinsic value"

Neither does a dollar bill. It's a piece of cotton-linen blend with ink on it. Its value comes from collective agreement - people accept it because other people accept it. Bitcoin has the same network effect, plus something the dollar doesn't: provable mathematical scarcity. Bitcoin is backed by the largest computing network in human history, consuming more energy than many countries. That energy expenditure is what makes it impossible to counterfeit or reverse. The dollar is backed by a promise from politicians. Which one sounds more reliable?

MYTH #2 "Bitcoin is only used by criminals"

The US dollar is used in more criminal activity than all cryptocurrencies combined - by orders of magnitude. Cash is untraceable; Bitcoin is the opposite. Every Bitcoin transaction is permanently recorded on a public ledger that anyone can inspect. Blockchain analytics firms like Chainalysis have helped law enforcement solve cases that would have been impossible with cash. According to Chainalysis, illicit activity represents less than 0.5% of all crypto transaction volume.[13] The criminal tool is cash, not Bitcoin.

MYTH #3 "Bitcoin wastes energy"

Bitcoin mining uses energy - on purpose. That's the cost of security. The question isn't "does it use energy?" but "is the energy well-spent?" And how much energy does the system it's replacing actually use?

🏦 Traditional Financial System

• ~260 TWh/year estimated total
• 80,000+ bank branches (US alone)
• Millions of ATMs worldwide
• Armored truck fleets, 24/7
• Massive data centers (Visa, SWIFT, etc.)
• Office towers in every city
• Central bank operations globally

⚡ Bitcoin Network

• ~150 TWh/year
• No branches, no buildings
• No armored trucks
• No employee overhead
• 50%+ sustainable energy[14]
• Captures stranded/wasted energy
• Secures $2T+ network, 24/7/365

The traditional financial system uses an estimated 2-3x more energy than Bitcoin - and that's before counting the military expenditure required to enforce the dollar's reserve currency status globally. Bitcoin does it all with math, electricity, and zero employees.

MYTH #4 "It's too volatile to be real money"

Bitcoin is volatile in dollar terms because it's a new asset being adopted on a global scale - the fastest monetary adoption in history. Zoom out: Bitcoin has gone from $0 to ~$100,000 in 16 years.[15] Every four-year cycle, its lows are higher than the previous cycle's highs. The volatility is the price of admission for a technology transitioning from zero to global reserve asset. Gold was volatile for decades during its monetary adoption too. The dollar itself lost 97% of its purchasing power - that's not stability, it's a slow, one-directional crash. Bitcoin's volatility is two-directional and decreasing over time as adoption grows.

MYTH #5 "Governments will just ban it"

They've tried. China banned Bitcoin mining and trading - twice. The hash rate recovered in weeks as miners moved to friendlier countries. India threatened a complete ban, then reversed course. Nigeria banned bank transfers to crypto exchanges; peer-to-peer Bitcoin adoption surged. El Salvador made it legal tender.[24] The US approved spot Bitcoin ETFs. You can't ban mathematics any more than you can ban the number 7. Bitcoin is a protocol - information transmitted between computers. Banning it would be like banning email. Governments that tried to ban the internet learned this lesson. Governments trying to ban Bitcoin are learning it now.

MYTH #6 "Bitcoin has no yield"

Show me yield and I'll show you risk. Every yield in finance comes from one place: someone else's promise to pay you back. A bond yields 4% because a government or corporation promises to repay - with money they may need to print or borrow more to cover. A savings account yields 0.5% because a bank is lending your money to someone else and hoping they don't default. DeFi "yield" often means your coins are being rehypothecated into layers of leveraged risk.

💰 "Yield" = Counterparty Risk

• Bond yield: gov't might inflate it away
• Bank savings: FDIC covers $250K, banks lend 90%
• Staking: protocol risk, slashing, smart contract bugs
• Lending: borrower default risk
• FTX "yield": turned out to be fraud

₿ Bitcoin = Zero Counterparty Risk

• No one can default on your Bitcoin
• No one can dilute your share of 21M
• No one can freeze or seize it
• ~55% CAGR over 10 years
• The "yield" is the world adopting it

Bitcoin doesn't need yield because it isn't someone else's liability. Gold doesn't have yield either - and it's been money for 5,000 years. The absence of yield is the absence of counterparty risk. That's not a bug. That's the entire point.

🎯 The Pattern

Every myth follows the same template: take a surface-level observation, ignore the deeper context, and present it as a dealbreaker. "It uses energy" ignores what the energy secures - and that the fiat system uses 2-3x more. "It's volatile" ignores the 16-year uptrend. "Criminals use it" ignores that cash is worse. "No intrinsic value" ignores that nothing has intrinsic value - value is always subjective. "Government will ban it" ignores that they can't. "No yield" ignores that yield always means someone else's risk. The myths aren't wrong because the observations are false. They're wrong because the conclusions are lazy.
