Mining

The lottery that secures the world's money

Mine a block yourself with real SHA-256 hashing in your browser and feel exactly what proof of work is. This is the reading version - the interactive lesson has the live demos, quizzes, and progress tracking.

Start the interactive lesson →

Bitcoin mining sounds complicated, but the basic idea is simple: miners are computers that compete to solve a mathematical puzzle to earn the right to add the next block. The first one to solve it wins a reward in Bitcoin - the block reward, currently 3.125 BTC per block[25].

Here's the genius: mining makes adding transactions expensive. To cheat, you'd need to redo all that expensive work - and faster than the entire rest of the network. This is called Proof of Work.

Modern mining uses dedicated chips called ASICs. Many miners are now located near cheap, renewable energy sources. A new block is found roughly every 10 minutes - by design.

Real energy. Real cost. Real work. That’s what makes Bitcoin unforgeable.

Every 210,000 blocks (~4 years), the block reward gets cut in half - the halving. It's built into Bitcoin's code from day one.

Key terms

mathematical puzzle
They're trying to find a number (called a "nonce") that, when combined with the block's data and run through a SHA-256 hash function, produces a hash starting with a certain number of zeros.
Proof of Work
The system where you prove you did hard mathematical work to earn the right to add a block. The work is expensive to do but cheap to verify.
ASICs
Application-Specific Integrated Circuit - a chip designed to do ONE thing: calculate Bitcoin hashes as fast as possible.
10 minutes
Bitcoin is designed to always target 10 minutes per block. If blocks come too fast, difficulty increases. Too slow, difficulty decreases.
halving
Bitcoin's supply schedule: every 4 years the new supply per block is halved. This continues until around 2140, when the last fraction of a Bitcoin will be mined. Total supply is capped at 21 million BTC, forever.
