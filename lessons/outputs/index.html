Outputs

UTXOs explained: why your wallet has coins instead of a balance, and how coin selection actually works.

Open the interactive lesson →

In Bitcoin, there's no such thing as a "balance." Instead, Bitcoin exists as UTXOs - Unspent Transaction Outputs. Think of it like a piggy bank full of physical coins and bills of different denominations.

When Alice sends 0.3 BTC to Bob, a new UTXO is created: "0.3 BTC locked to Bob's address." When Bob spends it later, he creates a new transaction that references this UTXO as input and creates new outputs. The old UTXO is "consumed."

🪙 Your UTXOs (Unspent Coins)

Your "balance" is just a pile of separate UTXOs. Like coins in a jar - each one is its own piece.

You must use whole UTXOs as inputs. If you have a 1 BTC UTXO and want to send 0.3 BTC, you spend the entire 1 BTC, create a 0.3 BTC output for the recipient and a ~0.699X BTC output back to yourself (change). Just like paying for a $3 coffee with a $10 bill.

A wallet with many small UTXOs creates large transactions when spending - and larger transactions cost more in fees. Consolidating small UTXOs during low-fee periods is called UTXO consolidation .

Your balance is a total your wallet calculates from separate unspent coins. Paying means spending whole coins and receiving change, exactly like handing over a banknote.
