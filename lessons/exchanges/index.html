Exchanges

How to buy bitcoin safely: choosing an exchange, understanding IOUs, and why coins on exchanges are promises.

Think of a Bitcoin exchange like an airport currency booth - except instead of swapping dollars for euros, you're swapping dollars for Bitcoin.

Exchanging is the oldest economic activity in human civilization. Long before money existed, humans gathered at crossroads, river forks, and market squares to trade what they had for what they needed - fish for firewood, grain for tools, labor for shelter. When gold and silver coins emerged, dedicated money changers set up tables at ports and bazaars, weighing coins from different kingdoms and converting them for a fee. A Bitcoin exchange is simply the digital evolution of this ancient practice - a platform where you trade one form of money (dollars, euros, pounds) for another form of money (Bitcoin). The difference? This time, the money you're buying can't be debased, counterfeited, or seized.

A cryptocurrency exchange is a website or app where you can buy Bitcoin using regular money (US dollars, euros, etc.). Popular ones include Coinbase , Kraken , and Binance . They work like stock exchanges - people post offers to buy or sell, and the platform matches buyers with sellers automatically.

To get started on an exchange, you typically need to create an account, verify your identity (called KYC - "Know Your Customer"), link your bank account or credit card, and then you can buy Bitcoin instantly. The exchange holds your Bitcoin for you - which is convenient but means you're trusting them. Many experts recommend withdrawing your Bitcoin to your own wallet after buying.

Exchanges charge fees for their services - usually a small percentage of each trade (like 0.5% to 1.5%). When comparing exchanges, look at fees, security history, and whether they're licensed in your country. A good rule: only use exchanges that are regulated and have a strong reputation. And remember - once you buy Bitcoin, consider moving it to YOUR own wallet. Your keys = your coins!

⚠️ Remember: When your Bitcoin sits on an exchange, they hold the private keys - not you. History is littered with exchanges that were hacked, went bankrupt, or froze withdrawals (Mt. Gox, FTX, Celsius). An exchange is a marketplace, not a vault. Buy on the exchange, then transfer to your own wallet. Not your keys, not your coins.
