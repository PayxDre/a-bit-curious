Sending

How sending Bitcoin works: addresses, fees, confirmations, and why transactions cannot be reversed.

Sending Bitcoin is like sending an email - you just need the recipient's address. No bank. No forms. No waiting 3-5 business days.

Sending Bitcoin is surprisingly simple. Every Bitcoin wallet has an address - a unique string of letters and numbers that identifies where to send coins. To send Bitcoin, you open your wallet app, paste the recipient's address, type the amount you want to send, choose a transaction fee , and press send. That's it.

Bitcoin transactions go through in minutes (usually 10-60 minutes for full confirmation), no matter where in the world you're sending. Sending $10,000 to Tokyo costs the same as sending $1 to your neighbor. Bitcoin doesn't care about geography, time zones, or weekends. This is revolutionary compared to traditional bank wires that can take days and cost $30-$50.

📤 How a Bitcoin Transaction Flows

One crucial tip: always double-check the address before sending! Bitcoin transactions are irreversible - there's no "undo" button and no customer service to call. A handy trick: always send a tiny test amount first, confirm the recipient got it, then send the full amount. Think of it like dialing a phone number and saying "Can you hear me?" before starting a long conversation.

A payment is a signed message that waits in a queue until a miner includes it in a block. A higher fee means a shorter wait. Once confirmed it cannot be undone, so the address is worth checking twice.
