Bitcoin mining sounds complicated, but the basic idea is simple: miners are computers that compete to solve a mathematical puzzle to earn the right to add the next block. The first one to solve it wins a reward in Bitcoin - the block reward, currently 3.125 BTC per block .

Here's the genius: mining makes adding transactions expensive. To cheat, you'd need to redo all that expensive work - and faster than the entire rest of the network. This is called Proof of Work .

Modern mining uses dedicated chips called ASICs . Many miners are now located near cheap, renewable energy sources. A new block is found roughly every 10 minutes - by design.

⛏ What It Takes to Mine a Block

Every 210,000 blocks (~4 years), the block reward gets cut in half - the halving . It's built into Bitcoin's code from day one.

Miners guess numbers until one produces a hash that starts with enough zeros. Finding it is expensive and checking it is instant. The winner adds the next block and earns new bitcoin, which is how coins are issued.
